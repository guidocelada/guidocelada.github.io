**Anti-Patterns Verdict**

The page does not immediately look AI-generated. Real address, hours, local photography, patient reviews, coverage names and preparation details make it credible. The weak spots are template-like section rhythm, many similar cards/accordions, generic section kickers and reviews sitting too high in the funnel.

The deterministic scan found 1 warning: `single-font` at `index.html:22`. This appears to be a false positive because the page loads Figtree and Lato, and CSS defines Lato for body plus Figtree for display.

Visual overlay was not produced. The browser evaluation surface rejected mutation during preflight, so no reliable user-visible overlay exists.

**Overall Impression**

The content is understandable for a low-context patient. The bigger issue is sequence. The page currently behaves like a landing page: hero, services, reviews, then practical information. For this audience, it should behave like a visit-planning helper: can I go, when, with what papers, does my coverage work, what if I need domicilio, how do I get results?

**What's Working**

The hero is strong: it quickly gives identity, hours, no-turno information, WhatsApp and address.

The service board is a good pattern for older users: big touch targets, familiar labels and clear navigation.

The coverage search is genuinely useful: visible label, examples, live count and a WhatsApp fallback.

**Priority Issues**

**[P1] Coberturas arrives too late**

Why it matters: Coverage is a go/no-go question. If a patient has PAMI, IOMA, OSDE or another plan, they often decide whether to visit based on coverage before reading preparation or reviews.

Fix: Move a compact coverage search directly after the service board. Keep the full list there or hide most of it behind search / "ver todas".

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

**[P1] Reviews interrupt the practical flow**

Why it matters: Reviews build trust, but users first need to know whether they can visit without wasting the trip. The full review block is currently the first major section after the service board.

Fix: Keep a small trust strip near the top, such as "+40 años, +50 opiniones, +10.000 pacientes por año", and move full reviews below the practical sections.

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`

**[P1] Studies and FAQ should become one "Antes de venir" system**

Why it matters: "Análisis de sangre", "¿Qué es el ayuno?", "¿Puedo tomar mate?" and "¿Necesito turno?" are one mental category for patients. Splitting them into study accordions plus a nested FAQ forces users to classify their own doubt.

Fix: Make one section called "Antes de venir" with grouped accordions: "Qué traer", "Ayuno", "Orina", "Estudios especiales", "Turnos y resultados".

Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Domicilio and resultados should appear before the long prep block**

Why it matters: Domicilio and results are top patient goals. They should not come after a long preparation/FAQ area.

Fix: After coverage, show two practical cards or split blocks: "Extracción a domicilio" and "Resultados".

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

**[P2] The hierarchy is too flat**

Why it matters: Older users scan big landmarks. Right now many sections have similar weight, so urgency is flattened.

Fix: Establish three tiers: immediate visit planning, before-coming instructions, proof/contact.

Suggested command: `$impeccable polish`

**Recommended Section Order**

1. Hero: address, hours, no-turno, WhatsApp, Maps.
2. Service board: Análisis, Preparación, Domicilio, Resultados, Coberturas.
3. Coberturas compact search: "¿Atendemos tu obra social?"
4. Domicilio + Resultados: two practical logistics blocks.
5. Antes de venir: merged studies and FAQ.
6. Reviews: full social proof after practical confidence.
7. Contact: final action area.

**Persona Red Flags**

Older patient: May understand each individual sentence, but has to scroll through reviews before coverage, and through many accordions before getting to coverage/contact.

Family helper: Needs a fast checklist for a parent: address, hours, coverage, what to bring, domicilio, results. Those answers exist but are scattered.

First-time visitor: The first-visit checklist is not a single object. DNI/order appears in preparation, coverage requirements appear in coverage, and no-turno appears in the hero and FAQ.

**Minor Observations**

"via email / whatsapp" should be "por email o WhatsApp".

"sin turno previo" should be capitalized if it starts a new line, or folded into the sentence.

The desktop service board CSS still uses six columns for four visible tiles, which creates awkward rhythm at wide widths.

The logo link touch target is about 39px high on mobile, below the 44px guideline.

Secondary pages are useful for compatibility and SEO, but they feel like thin doorway pages. They are acceptable as transition pages, but should not become the main experience.

**Questions to Consider**

What if the homepage's first job were "avoid a wasted trip" instead of "introduce the lab"?

Can the first two screenfuls answer: "¿Voy? ¿Cuándo? ¿Con qué papeles? ¿Me cubre?"

Would a "Primera vez en el laboratorio" checklist outperform separate FAQ and preparation sections?
